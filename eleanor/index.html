Muscle recovery

Estimated from what you logged: sets, how close to failure you went, hours since, soreness and how your next session went. Tap a muscle for the detail.

How it's worked out

Worked Good evidence 3+ hard sets on a muscle keep it "worked" for 48 hours, or 72 hours if a set went within 1 rep of failure or the exercise is new to you. Strength and power take 24–48 hours longer to come back after training to failure (Morán-Navarro et al. 2017), and unfamiliar exercises cause more damage the first time (the repeated-bout effect).

Recovering Moderate Past that window but you're still showing soreness: "very" sore legs in this morning's check-in, "bit" sore within 4 days, "still sore" logged going into the last session for that muscle, or an ache in the matching joint. Soreness alone doesn't measure damage (Nosaka 2008), so it only slows the call, it never makes it.

Ready Everything else. The real test is your next session: matching or beating last time's reps at the same weight shows the muscle recovered, and each muscle's card shows how you did.

Watch flags Moderate A muscle gets a red Watch flag when it's pushed past what helps: more weekly sets than the top of its range (gains flatten above roughly 20 sets a week, Pelland et al. 2024), trained again before it came out of its window twice in two weeks, or reps dropped last session while it was still sore. None of these means overtrained; they are early signs of not recovering, and your coach trims that muscle first.

The bar shows hours since you last trained it against its 48 or 72-hour window, not a recovery percentage.

Not a measurement. No app can measure recovery in one muscle; this is an estimate from your logs, kept to three honest states instead of a made-up percentage.

Fresh exercises

Why and when

Only when it's earned Moderate Rotating exercises every few weeks can add a little extra muscle growth and keeps training fresh, but changing them too often slows strength gains while you relearn the movement (Kassiano et al., systematic review, 2022). So a swap is only suggested when a lift hasn't progressed in 3 sessions in a row (best estimated 1RM, or reps for bodyweight moves) or keeps getting skipped.

Same muscles, one at a time The new exercise trains the same muscles as the old one. It gets at least 6 weeks before it can be rotated again, and no more than 2 swaps run at once. "Keep" hides the idea for 3 weeks; "Switch back" restores the original.

Your coach sees it Swaps show in your plan straight away, and your 6:30am coach builds your sessions around them. It may also suggest one itself from your notes and trends.

Readiness

How the call is made

Go / Trim / Recover. Each warning sign below counts once. One or more means trim: one less set per lift and no increases, or a shorter run. Recover means mobility and a walk, and needs a strong signal (you tap Wrecked, or resting HR jumps 8+ bpm) or three signs at once.

HRV Good evidence Your 7-day rolling average is compared with your own normal range (mean ± 0.5 SD of the last 30 days, log-transformed), the method used in HRV-guided training studies. Needs 10 mornings; until then a single morning 25% under your median counts.

Resting HR Moderate 5+ bpm over your baseline with a rising 7-day average trims; an 8+ bpm jump in one morning is treated as possible illness and means recover.

How you feel Good evidence Self-reported fatigue, soreness and stress track training stress better than most objective markers (Saw et al., BJSM 2016). Tired, very sore legs or high stress each count. Very sore legs don't count on an Upper day, since that session barely loads them.

Tired, but your numbers are fine Coach's call If you tap Tired or Meh while your HRV, resting HR and Garmin scores are all in range and you slept 6+ hours, it's treated as a motivation dip, not fatigue: the full session stands, with a 10-minute rule (start the warm-up; still flat after 10 minutes, switch to Express).

Drinks Moderate Alcohol lowers overnight HRV and sleep quality and blunts muscle protein synthesis after training (Parr et al. 2014). 5+ drinks trims the session; 3–4 adds a heads-up only.

Sleep under 6 h Moderate Sleep loss hurts endurance and volume work more than single heavy sets.

Body Battery under 30, sleep score under 50, Training Readiness under 50 Garmin's own scores No published thresholds exist, so these can only trim, never cancel a session.

Getting stronger Good evidence Hit the top of the rep range on every set and the weight goes up; reps in reserve guide effort. Legs get 12–16 hard sets a week (dose-response meta-analyses), upper body is held at maintenance.
